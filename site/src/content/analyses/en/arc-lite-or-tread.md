## Our view in brief

Keep the €100 and take the Arc Lite if the oven will mainly stay on a garden
table. It is lighter, its opening is slightly wider, and an eight-pizza session
shows it keeping pace with a relaxed evening without any particular wait.

Choose the Tread if you will genuinely load it into a car, place it on its
portable stand or bake pizzas in close succession. Its handles, better-retained
stone and more practical gas connection then serve every outing. Two filmed
comparisons also see it reach their target temperature faster than the Arc
Lite.

Yet the Tread is not the lightweight choice: it weighs 13.5 kg against the Arc
Lite's 12 kg. The extra €100 buys better transport organisation and, in the
available sessions, faster recovery. It buys neither a larger pizza nor proof
of a better bake.

## The €100 does not save weight

On 24 August 2026, Gozney France displayed the
[Arc Lite at €399.99](https://eu.gozney.com/fr/products/arc-lite) and the
[Tread at €499.99](https://eu.gozney.com/fr/products/tread). Both run on
propane, remain for outdoor use only and have a removable 12 mm cordierite
floor.

| Checked price | €399.99 | €499.99 |
| Oven weight | 12 kg | 13.5 kg |

The Tread is slightly narrower and deeper. It gives no extra width for turning
a pizza. Its advantage lies around the oven: upper grips, stone retention, hose
storage and a transport ecosystem. One filmed comparison shows these details
side by side. The Arc Lite is carried by grips beneath its chassis and keeps
its hose attached.

Make this practical. If the oven moves from garage to terrace, both remain
transportable. If it must travel often, the Tread is easier to handle and carry.
Its stand, bag and accessories remain separate purchases: compare
the complete basket, not just the two bare ovens.

## The Tread heats faster in two sessions

Grill Top Experience placed probes stated to tolerate 1,200°F inside both
ovens. In this session, the Tread reaches 700°F, around 371°C, in 14 min 30 sec,
against 20 minutes for the Arc Lite
([watch from 03:54](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eutsY8rdNow&t=234s)). The
session takes place in wind, the exact probe positions are not detailed, and
the gas bottles are swapped during the check. The video also contains affiliate
links.

Some Dads Cook publishes a second head-to-head comparison. Its threshold is
higher: 850°F, around 454°C. The Tread reaches it in 23 minutes and the Arc Lite
in 36 minutes
([watch from 03:30](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KhqmMgbv_SY&t=210s)). The
author tries three bottles on the Arc Lite and says he obtains the same result.
However, he publishes neither the outdoor temperature nor the exact sensor
position.

Both sessions point in the same direction: the Tread reaches each author's
chosen threshold faster. We do not mix their timings. Target temperatures,
weather, instruments and setups differ, so an average would create misleading
precision.

## Recovery matters most when everyone is waiting

The most informative check comes from the second video. Both floors remain at
850°F for twenty minutes before a pizza is baked for 90 seconds. Afterwards,
the Tread returns to the threshold in 2 min 45 sec; the Arc Lite takes 7 minutes
([watch from 05:12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KhqmMgbv_SY&t=312s)).

That difference is useful if four dough balls are already waiting in
[suitable boxes](/en/pizza-accessories/dough-boxes-guide/) on the table. But it
comes from a single pizza in each oven. The dough, weather and repeatability of
the series are not described well enough to promise the same delay at home.

At a more family-oriented pace, the Arc Lite looks less pressured. The Barbecue
Lab baked eight pizzas from 23 to almost 30 cm after thirty minutes of heating.
The floor remains between 750 and 800°F at launch, and the author says he never
waited for the oven. He also specifies that the rhythm was relaxed, without a
race for throughput.

In other words, do not pay for Tread recovery to solve an urgency you do not
have. If everyone tops their pizza between bakes, the Arc Lite may have time to
recover its heat. If the dough is ready and service must move, the Tread has a
documented advantage, still limited to one session.

## For a first evening, aim closer to 25–28 cm

Both ovens claim 30 cm. This describes what fits, not necessarily the easiest
diameter to turn on the first evening.

On the Arc Lite, The Barbecue Lab finds 25 to 28 cm pizzas easier to handle. The
largest in the series require pulling the pizza back out with the
[launching peel](/en/pizza-accessories/pizza-peel-guide/) and turning it by
hand. This account covers one author, dough at 63% hydration and one oven. We
treat it as handling advice, not a new product limit.

The Tread has an official opening of 310 mm, 15 mm less than the Arc Lite. Its
chamber is deeper, however. No sufficiently precise source establishes that it
is easier or harder to turn the same pizza. In both cases, starting around 25
to 28 cm simply leaves more room for the movement.

If that diameter already looks too small, do not force this comparison. The
[Gozney Arc XL](/en/gozney/arc-xl/) answers another question: gaining genuine
space around 30 to 35 cm pizzas in a heavier, fixed installation.

## The Tread cools a little faster, not instantly

Mobility begins after baking. The
[Arc Lite manual](https://eu.gozney.com/cdn/shop/files/Arc_Lite_Manual_-_UK_EU.pdf?v=10531609776578512990)
requires at least thirty minutes before moving the oven. In the Grill Top
Experience session, the author chooses 150°F on the stone as a personal
threshold before loading: the Tread reaches it in 38 minutes, the Arc Lite in
43.

Those five minutes do not promise a quick departure in every condition. They
mainly show that “portable” does not mean “put away just after the final
pizza”. Include cooling in the evening and follow your appliance manual before
touching or loading it.

## Our choice for your evening

We would take the Arc Lite for a terrace, two to six pizzas and a rhythm where
guests are still topping their dough while the previous pizza bakes. It costs
less, weighs less and its opening offers a little more width.

We would pay for the Tread for frequent travel, its carrying system and tighter
series. Faster heating appears in two direct comparisons, and its recovery
saves more than four minutes in one session. That is enough to change how a
service is organised, not to call the oven better in absolute terms.

Before ordering, budget for a launching peel, a turning peel, an
[infrared thermometer](/en/pizza-accessories/infrared-thermometer-guide/), a
cover and a stable table. For the Tread, add only the transport accessories you
will genuinely use. An “adventure” oven that never leaves the garden is mostly
an Arc Lite bought at a higher price.

## What the sources do not yet tell us

We have not tried these ovens ourselves. The available comparisons cover few
units, use different protocols and carry disclosed paid links. They do not
measure gas consumption, noise, lifespan or comfort after ten journeys.

Our preference for the Arc Lite at home and the Tread on the move is an
editorial reading based on price, transport practicality and published timings.
It must be reconsidered if your priority is a large pizza, a different fuel or
a wider permanent installation.
