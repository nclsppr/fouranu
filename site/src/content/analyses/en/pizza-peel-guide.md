## The short answer: measure the oven before looking at the brand

If you only want to buy one peel, I would start with a **30 cm aluminium
peel**, either solid or perforated, provided it passes through the oven opening
with a comfortable margin. The solid version is the easiest to understand. The
perforated version lets some flour fall away before launching. Bamboo is pleasant
for preparation and serving, but it is not the tool I would choose for working
close to a flame.

No peel is therefore best in absolute terms. It either suits or does not suit a
pizza diameter, an oven opening and a particular movement. That is one of the
details I like about making pizza: the most impressive tool is not necessarily
the one that makes the movement cleanest.

finding somewhere for the utensil once the evening is over.

## Ooni perforated 30 cm: to reduce the flour that falls onto the stone

The [Ooni perforated peel product page](https://eu.ooni.com/fr/collections/essentials/products/ooni-perforated-pizza-peel?variant=41754553647268)
lists an anodised-aluminium head, a glass-reinforced nylon handle and a weight
of 585 g. The 30 cm version measures 65.9 × 30.5 × 3 cm. Its edge is tapered,
and the openings in the head are intended to release flour and steam.

This is the model I would look at first for soft dough dusted with flour just
before launching. It does not replace good technique: you still need to place
the topped pizza on the peel late, check that it slides and launch without
waiting. The perforations simply give some of the flour an exit before it ends
up on the hot stone.

Size remains its real limitation. A head sold as 30 cm is not designed to carry
a 35 or 40 cm pizza comfortably. Ooni also requires hand washing and indoor
storage, and advises against prolonged exposure to high heat. A peel enters the
oven for one movement; it does not stay there.

## Ooni classic 30.5 cm: the solid peel for learning

The [Ooni classic pizza peel](https://eu.ooni.com/fr/products/ooni-pizza-peel)
uses a lightweight aluminium plate and the same kind of glass-reinforced nylon
handle. The manufacturer gives dimensions of 30.5 × 66 × 3.5 cm and a weight
of 530 g for the 30 cm version.

Its solid surface is easy to read when you are starting out. You can see the
dough, make a small back-and-forth movement to check that it is not sticking,
then launch. It can also retrieve the baked pizza. On the other hand, all the
flour placed beneath the dough remains on the plate unless you shake it away.

I would recommend it to someone who prefers a straightforward tool and wants to
work on their launch before adding a turning peel. I would only avoid using its
surface as a preparation board for several minutes: the longer topped dough
waits on a metal peel, the more opportunity it has to stick.

## Ooni bamboo 30 cm: a versatile board and a more specialised peel

The [Ooni bamboo peel and board](https://eu.ooni.com/fr-fr/products/ooni-wooden-pizza-peel)
measures 50.5 × 30.5 × 1.2 cm and weighs 717 g in its 30 cm version. Ooni
describes it as a surface for launching, serving and
[cutting](/en/pizza-accessories/pizza-scissors-guide/). The bamboo is presented
as moisture-resistant.

Its appeal is obvious in a small kitchen: it can hold the dough, carry the pizza
to the oven and return to the table as a board. But that versatility stops at
the flame. It is thicker than the aluminium models, and the manufacturer says
that it is not designed for prolonged exposure to extreme temperatures.

I therefore see it as a good **launching and serving peel**, not as the only
peel for someone who wants to turn a pizza quickly in a very hot oven. It must
be hand-washed and allowed to dry completely before storage.

## Weber 6691: folding stainless steel for the cupboard

The [Weber 6691 product page](https://www.weber.com/FR/fr/accessoires/cuisson/ustensiles-de-grillades/6691.html)
lists a stainless-steel peel measuring 57 × 31.5 × 4.3 cm. Its non-slip rubber
handle pivots to reduce its footprint, and the whole utensil is dishwasher-safe.

That is not a minor detail. A peel more than 60 cm long quickly ends up against
a wall, behind a door or in a garage. If your oven only comes out a few times a
month and every accessory has to go back into a cupboard, the Weber's folding
handle solves a real problem.

The trade-off is a solid stainless-steel head and a size mostly suited to pizzas
around 30 cm. I would prefer a longer handle for a large, deep oven. For a
domestic oven, barbecue or stone placed near the opening, its compact size may
matter more than the difference in material.

## A wide peel does not always replace a turning peel

Ooni's [overview of the different types of peel](https://eu.ooni.com/fr-fr/pages/explore-pizza-peels)
distinguishes the classic or perforated peel used for launching and retrieving
from the small round peel used to turn the pizza inside the oven.

You can learn to turn with a large peel by pulling the pizza slightly towards
you. This requires space in front of the oven and slows the movement. A turning
peel becomes useful when the bake is short, the flame colours one side faster,
or several pizzas need to follow one another.

For a first set of equipment, I would nevertheless keep this order:

1. a wide peel that can genuinely launch and retrieve the pizza;
2. a [suitable thermometer](/en/pizza-accessories/infrared-thermometer-guide/)
   if the oven does not measure the stone;
3. a turning peel once the need appears in your technique.

Buying both peels immediately is not essential. Finding yourself with a hot
oven and no safe way to place the dough inside it is a different matter.

## The five measurements to take before ordering

- **The usable oven opening.** Measure its width at the narrowest point, not
  just the advertised diameter of the stone.
- **The actual diameter of your pizzas.** A 30 cm pizza needs a head close to
  30 cm; some margin beneath the dough is reassuring when launching.
- **The depth of the oven.** This determines a comfortable handle length.
- **The space in front of the opening.** You need to be able to draw the peel
  back without hitting a table, a wall or a guest.
- **Storage.** Also measure the cupboard, hook or box that will hold the tool
  when it is cold and clean.

Ooni's [guide to choosing a peel](https://eu.ooni.com/fr-fr/blogs/ooni-insights/how-to-pick-the-right-pizza-peel)
repeats the most useful principle: a 30 cm peel suits a 30 cm oven, while a
larger oven can accept several widths. The reverse does not work. A 40 cm peel
does not magically fit through a 30 cm opening.

## What I would choose

For a domestic oven and pizzas around 28 to 30 cm, I would choose the Ooni
perforated peel if the budget and storage allow. Its purpose is not to promise a
better result; it is to remove a little flour before it burns on the stone.

I would take the Ooni classic to learn with a solid surface, the bamboo board
for preparation and serving, and the Weber when every centimetre of cupboard
space counts. They are four coherent answers to four different constraints.
The sources do not allow us to rank them for durability, comfort or baking
quality under identical conditions.
