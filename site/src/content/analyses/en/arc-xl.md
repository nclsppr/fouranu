## Our view in brief

If you mainly make 30 to 35 cm pizzas and the oven can remain outside, the Arc
XL has a genuine benefit. Its large floor leaves room to turn without working
to the millimetre against the flame.

A 40 cm pizza also fits. That is the attractive promise, but it is not the
easiest size to bake. The two available accounts show that, at this diameter,
the dough gets close to the burner and needs to be turned early.

The price displayed in France was €899.99 on 24 August 2026. Add a solid and
sufficiently deep table, peels and a cover. The oven's 26.5 kg also call for a
stable location. The purchase makes more sense for a lasting pizza station
than for an oven brought out of the garage on Sundays.

## Before diameter, look at the available space

The chassis is 53 cm wide and 62.9 cm deep. Inside, Gozney states dimensions of
42.7 × 51.7 cm and a removable 20 mm cordierite floor. This depth lets you keep
the pizza away from the flame during the bake.

The oven itself tells only part of the footprint story. The manual requires at
least 30 cm between the appliance and combustible materials behind and at the
sides, then 100 cm in front. It also prohibits placing the Arc XL beneath or on
a combustible structure.

In plain terms, do not choose a table flush with the chassis. Allow for
clearances, the gas bottle and your movements in front of the opening. At
26.5 kg, we would give it a fixed position rather than move it for every bake.

## A 40 cm pizza fits, but without much margin

Gozney claims pizzas up to 40 cm. The Barbecue Lab fits one inside, but its
centre remains undercooked after starting on a perforated baking support,
called a “screen” in the video. A direct finish on the floor is then attempted.
The source attributes part of the problem to this support. One pizza is not
enough to judge the oven.

The Tom's Guide account mainly confirms the difficulty of the movement. After
a month with pizzas from 30 to 40 cm, the author explains that the largest come
very close to the flame. They must be turned early, while the base may still
lack structure and tear.

If 40 cm is your priority, plan for a [suitable peel](/en/pizza-accessories/pizza-peel-guide/)
and some learning. For more common family use, we would choose the Arc XL for
the margin it gives around a 30 to 35 cm pizza. That is where its large format
becomes genuinely comfortable.

## How long before the first pizza?

Tom's Guide reports a floor close to 400°C after 15 to 20 minutes. In The
Barbecue Lab session, an infrared thermometer reads about 427°C at the centre
after 30 minutes, with points ranging from roughly 331 to 484°C across the
floor.

Do not turn those two timings into a guaranteed record. Weather, starting
temperature, measuring points and flame setting do not follow the same
protocol. To organise a meal, retain a reference range of 15 to 30 minutes in
these two sessions, then check your own floor.

Recovery looks more reassuring in the filmed series. After a 30 cm pizza baked
in about 90 seconds, the floor loses close to 42°C and returns above 427°C in
under two minutes. The series contains eight pizzas. It shows rapid succession
in those conditions, not a throughput guaranteed in every weather.

## The display helps; the infrared thermometer decides

The manual says that the display shows the temperature “in the oven” and gives
ranges by pizza style. It does not explain precisely enough how this value
relates to different areas of the stone.

The two third-party sources do not give it the same meaning either. The
Barbecue Lab believes it mainly indicates when the stone has finished storing
heat. Tom's Guide reads it as an ambient temperature. In the video, several
infrared readings across the floor differ markedly from the displayed value.

We would therefore use the display to follow the rise in temperature. Before
launching, an [infrared thermometer](/en/pizza-accessories/infrared-thermometer-guide/)
tells you directly what is happening where the dough will touch the stone.

## The side flame does not let you walk away

The burner sits at the side and its flame rolls beneath the dome. In the series
of eight 30 cm pizzas, the filmed source turns each pizza four or five times
during a total bake of about 90 seconds. The large chamber gives you room, but
you must stay in front of the oven.

The Detroit-style pizza tells the same story over a longer bake. A first
protocol burns the bottom. On the second attempt, the pan remains away from the
burner and bakes for five minutes on each side. The result appears more even.
For this kind of recipe, the position of the pan matters as much as the flame
setting.

## Who we would choose it for

We would choose the Arc XL for frequent outdoor gas cooking, 30 to 35 cm pizzas
and a proper working area. It then offers the margin missing from tighter
ovens, without forcing you to target the maximum diameter at every service.

We would pass if the oven must come inside after every use, if the table only
just accepts its 62.9 cm depth, or if a wood fire is part of the pleasure you
want. The Arc XL runs on gas and remains for outdoor use only.

The floor is described as removable, but Tom's Guide reports that removal is
difficult without the intended kit. The author had to tilt the oven and work
with another person. If you fear failed launches or want to clean beneath the
stone, check the price and availability of that kit before purchase.

## The list to check before ordering

- A stable table that supports at least 26.5 kg, excluding accessories.
- The 30 cm rear and side clearances, followed by one clear metre in front.
- A lasting outdoor place for the oven and its propane bottle.
- A peel that suits the diameter you actually make.
- An infrared thermometer to check several points on the floor.
- The full budget with stand, cover, launching peel and turning peel.

## What the sources do not yet tell us

Our reading brings together the current French product page, the European
manual and two accounts of use. These sources do not support a clean comparison
with the entire Gozney range or the large Ooni ovens. They also cover few units
and measure neither repeated consumption, ageing nor after-sales service in
France.

The preference stated here for 30 to 35 cm pizzas is an editorial inference
based on the observed room for turning. Gozney does not publish this limit, and
Four à Nu has not measured it.
