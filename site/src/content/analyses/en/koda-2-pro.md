## Plenty of room, but not for everyone

The Koda 2 Pro targets 45 cm pizzas and weighs 30 kg. Its U-shaped burner wraps
around the stone on three sides, but a single dial controls the entire flame.
We find it coherent for a large gas setup. For 30 cm pizzas, it looks frankly
oversized.

The large opening gives you room to turn the pizza. It also requires a deeper
table, proper clearances and a fixed position. You will not willingly move a
30 kg appliance between bakes.

## A U-shaped flame does not remove the need to turn

The flame wraps around the rear and both sides. Published sessions show that it
colours the crust quickly. You still have to turn the pizza. The centre of the
stone remains the easiest area to read with an
[infrared thermometer](/en/pizza-accessories/infrared-thermometer-guide/).

One dial controls both branches of the burner. The setting is simpler than two
zones, but cannot correct one side separately. This architecture is better
suited to one large pizza than two pizzas baked in parallel.

## Published times do not create a promise

One session reads around 400°C at the centre after 25 minutes. Another exceeds
500°C on the stone after at least 45 minutes, but its timing was interrupted.
These two readings cannot establish an average heating time.

The published bakes show a Neapolitan pizza, a larger pizza and regular turns.
They visually confirm high-temperature baking. They do not measure the failure
rate, recovery after several pizzas, or ease for a beginner.

The connected temperature hub and food probe can be useful for other dishes.
For Neapolitan pizza, an infrared thermometer remains the most direct way to
read the stone temperature.

## Who can justify this large format

We would choose it for a fixed outdoor position, pizzas from 35 to 45 cm and a
cook already comfortable with turning. It can also make sense for large dishes
used regularly.

We would rule it out if the oven must be stored away, if most pizzas are under
32 cm, or if simultaneous baking is the priority. The
[Koda 2](/en/ooni/koda-2/) is easier to place. The
[Koda 2 Max](/en/ooni/koda-2-max/) better answers the need for two zones.

## Before giving it a table

- Choose a table that can carry 30 kg with a safety margin.
- Measure the total depth, including the hose and serving movements.
- Compare the actual size of your [peel](/en/pizza-accessories/pizza-peel-guide/)
  and dough balls.
- Add a suitable cover and accessories to the budget.
- Ask how often the 45 cm format will genuinely be used.

## What this article supports

The Koda 2 Pro appears most coherent for large pizzas and a fixed position. The
cited videos use different doughs, temperatures and rhythms. They do not show
how many pizzas the oven can produce consistently in succession. Our
conclusion concerns the relationship between the large format, U-shaped flame
and footprint, without claiming first-hand Four à Nu experience.
