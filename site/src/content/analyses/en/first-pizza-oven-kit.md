## The kit I would put together before looking at bundles

**A peel that fits the oven, a way to check the stone and suitable containers for the dough: that is where I would start.** Scissors, a second peel and large dough boxes can wait. I would rather buy a small, coherent selection than a bundle with half its contents left in a cupboard.

This guide helps assemble equipment; it does not suggest buying all four products at once. Each reference below answers a specific need. Our choices use published specifications and our detailed guides, without physical testing by Four à Nu. The Amazon.fr listings were identified on **30 August 2026**; check the current offer before ordering. The market remains France.

Dimensions come from the manufacturers. This selection is neither a quality ranking nor a guarantee that these accessories fit every oven.

## First purchase: a peel, if it is actually missing

I would choose a solid peel to start with a straightforward tool. The **Ooni classic 30 cm** uses an aluminium plate; the manufacturer states an actual width of 30.5 cm. The oven’s name and stone diameter are therefore insufficient: measure the usable opening and allow room for the handle’s movement in front of the appliance.

A larger pizza calls for another size. A head that only just squeezes through is no reason to keep this reference. Our [peel guide](/en/pizza-accessories/pizza-peel-guide/) also compares perforated, bamboo and folding-handle options.

Read the box contents before adding anything. **Cuisinart already lists a folding peel with the CPZ120E**, along with a stone, deep pan and cutter. In that situation, I would start with the supplied equipment rather than automatically buy a second set.

## The thermometer: useful for a specific question

An infrared thermometer answers one question: what is the temperature of the surface being aimed at? It does not replace a food probe or directly measure the air inside the oven. It can help track the stone, but it does not select the right temperature for you.

The **Ooni model** has a clear rationale for an Ooni cordierite stone: the manufacturer states that it is preset for that material. I would not turn that into a universal recommendation for every stone. If you also want to measure other materials, our [thermometer comparison](/en/pizza-accessories/infrared-thermometer-guide/) explains the settings and their limits.

## The box: measure the fridge, not just the dough

The **ZiiPa Minola** is an option if its dimensions fit your fridge. The brand lists up to six 250 g dough balls; that is a manufacturer capacity, dependent on dough weight and proofing. I would leave room to remove the lid and lift out a dough ball without disturbing its neighbours.

Already own suitable food containers? Keep them if their size and closure work for your preparation. Our [dough-box guide](/en/pizza-accessories/dough-boxes-guide/) mainly helps avoid buying a professional-size box that cannot be stored at home.

## Scissors: a serving choice, not a requirement for starting

**Dreamfarm Scizza** combines separable blades and a protective base. Its purpose is cutting and serving, depending on the surface used. It should not touch a hot pizza stone: the manufacturer rates the base to 205 °C, not to a pizza oven’s maximum temperature.

I would add it only if you like that serving method or your current tool is unsuitable. Our [scissors guide](/en/pizza-accessories/pizza-scissors-guide/) compares shapes and cleaning; it does not establish that one model cuts better than another.

## My buying order

1. Check the exact oven, its installation and its box contents.
2. Add only the missing peel, in the right width.
3. Decide whether surface measurements and dedicated boxes meet a real need.
4. Keep serving tools for the convenience you actually want.
