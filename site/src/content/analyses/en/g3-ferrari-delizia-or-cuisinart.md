## My choice would depend on the handling you want to learn

**I would consider the G3 Ferrari Delizia for a dedicated, hinged-lid appliance, provided you accept following its heating cycle. I would choose the Cuisinart CPZ120E if the illuminated window and included equipment matter more.** In either case, “400 °C” is not enough to decide.

This comparison concerns a first purchase: how to launch a pizza, watch what happens and avoid duplicate accessories. It does not rank cooking quality. The sources did not test both exact references under one protocol, and Four à Nu has not carried out physical testing.

Diameters do not describe external dimensions. I would measure the worktop and storage space before concluding that one fits better. Manual clearances matter just as much as the appliance body.

## Why I might choose the Delizia

The Delizia answers a specific wish: a dedicated pizza appliance with a stone and controls to learn. For the black `G10006 10`, G3 Ferrari lists 1,200 W, a 31 cm stone, a five-minute timer and a thermostat up to 400 °C. Neither the timer nor the maximum temperature proves your pizza will cook in five minutes.

The trade-off is following the cycle. Pizzaschaufel.ch describes thermostat fluctuations on its grey unit and awkward launching with two half-peels. El País finds those half-peels useful on another variant. **I see handling to learn, not a universal verdict on the accessories.** The unit references do not allow direct attribution of those results to the black model offered on Amazon.fr.

I would therefore keep the Delizia if this operation suits you and you are willing to check the supplied equipment. I would reject it if you expect documented temperature stability or a device the sources support using without monitoring.

## Why I might prefer the Cuisinart

The CPZ120E has an argument that is easy to include in a first basket: Cuisinart already lists a stone, folding peel, deep pan and cutter. Its illuminated glass door also gives a view of the pizza. I would begin with those accessories before buying another peel or cutting tool.

In return, our [Cuisinart guide](/en/pizza-ovens/cuisinart-cpz120e/) reports a long warm-up, imprecise dial and audible ventilation from Les Numériques’ session with the CPZ120E. These are one source’s observations of one unit, not a defect rate. They are still enough to rule it out if silence or a very quick start is a priority.

I would choose it for straightforward organisation and included equipment, not on the assumption that it cooks better than the Delizia. The evidence does not support that comparison.

## The decision I would make before ordering

- **A hinged lid and learning the cycle suit you:** open the [Delizia guide](/en/pizza-ovens/g3-ferrari-delizia-g10006/) and first check the offer’s condition.
- **The window and supplied accessories simplify your plan:** keep the CPZ120E if you accept its heating and noise limitations.
- **More specialised controls are the priority:** our [Volt 2, Sage and Cuisinart comparison](/en/pizza-ovens/volt-2-sage-or-cuisinart/) covers that different decision.

Then add only what is missing with our [first-oven kit guide](/en/pizza-ovens/first-pizza-oven-kit/). I would choose neither while installation space and actual box contents remain unclear.
